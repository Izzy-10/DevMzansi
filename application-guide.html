Your next step starts here

Apply with a little more confidence.

You don’t need years of experience to make a strong application. Be clear about what you can do, show how you learn, and make it easy for an employer to follow up.

01

Read the listing carefully

Check the closing date, location, work arrangement, eligibility and application link. Confirm the role is still open on the employer’s own site before sharing personal details.

02

Make your CV easy to scan

Keep it concise and tailored to the role. Put your strongest relevant projects, tools and achievements near the top, with links to work you can show.

  • Use clear headings and consistent dates.
  • Describe your contribution, not just the project.
  • Check spelling, contact details and link permissions.
03

Show your work

A small finished project can say a lot. Link to a live demo or repository, explain the problem you solved, and mention what you learned or would improve next.

04

Write a focused message

Use a specific subject line, name the role, briefly explain why you’re interested, and attach only what the listing asks for. Follow the employer’s instructions first.

05

Prepare for the conversation

Practise explaining your projects in plain language. Be ready to talk through a challenge, how you approached it, and what you would do differently.

06

Follow up professionally

If the listing gives a response timeframe, respect it. Otherwise, one polite follow-up after a reasonable interval is enough. Keep applying while you wait.

Before you press send

  • I meet the stated eligibility requirements.
  • My CV and project links open correctly.
  • I used the employer’s real application channel.
  • I have not sent passwords, banking details or unnecessary identity documents.
